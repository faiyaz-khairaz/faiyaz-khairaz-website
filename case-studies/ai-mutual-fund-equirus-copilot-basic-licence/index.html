Don't teach random prompts. Build role-based agents.

Copilot Chat on the basic licence doesn't keep your uploaded files from one chat to the next. So the program focused on what does carry over: a reusable role, a reusable instruction set and a reusable output style.

Example agent instructionsRole: You are a mutual fund business analyst for our leadership team. Input: I will upload an Excel workbook (distribution, fund flows or client profitability). Do: List the sheets and what each one contains. Then give the five most important findings, each with the number that supports it and the sheet it came from. Check: State any assumption you make, such as how AUM or net flows are defined. If two sheets disagree, say so. Output: An executive summary in 5 bullets, a table of key figures, and 3 recommended actions. Never: give investment, legal or regulatory advice.

Why it works: the agent behaves the same way every time, so the whole team gets consistent, comparable outputs.

Copilot builds fast.
Training teaches what to verify.

Every dashboard agreed on net flows: ₹1,048.4 Cr. But the AUM figure ranged from ₹6,432 Cr to ₹91,989 Cr, because each team defined AUM differently: a period-end snapshot, a sum of rows, or a client-level total. None of them was "wrong". Each was a definition someone had to choose. That conversation was one of the most useful moments of the day.

Define the measure

Tell Copilot exactly how AUM or net flows should be calculated.

Ask for the source

Every number should come with the sheet it came from.

Compare outputs

When two answers differ, find out why before sharing.

Own the decision

Copilot drafts. People decide what goes to leadership.
